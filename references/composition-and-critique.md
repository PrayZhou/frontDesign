# Composition and critique

## From material to a first screen

Lay out the material the visitor must interpret. On an operations page this might mean an account name, the blocked work, its deadline and one real action. On an exhibition page it might mean two images whose meaning depends on being seen together. On a long read it might mean a title with a meaningful line break, a short opening paragraph and a figure whose caption belongs at a specific point in the argument.

Identify the consequence of the first screen. A supervisor who needs to call one account benefits from having the reason for the call adjacent to the account link. A supervisor reviewing how the queue was ranked benefits from seeing the first two accounts on the same axes. In the second brief, a large featured account could consume the space needed for comparison.

## Two comparable sketches

Use a pair of low-cost thumbnails, browser frames, or simple labeled text wireframes when graphical tools are unavailable. Hold the viewport, copy, pictures and data constant. Draw the size and placement of the lead item and its relation to the next item. A list of style names leaves those choices unresolved.

For each sketch, finish these sentences in concrete terms:

- My eye lands on ___ because of its size, crop, position or contrast.
- I can follow ___ to ___ without losing the relationship.
- This leaves less room for ___, which matters when ___.

Choose one direction and keep the useful alternative visible in the design handoff. For a supplied layout or a localized fix, mark the constraint and go straight to the relevant detail. Avoid inventing extra interface copy to explain the sketch.

## Critique a supplied image

Work outward from the observed problem. For example, at 320px a figure caption may start closer to the next heading than to its own photograph. Identify those two gaps, explain the possible misreading, then propose keeping the picture and caption together inside one figure and moving the larger gap below it. If implementing the change, revisit the same crop. For feedback alone, this becomes the proposed check. The screenshot can support an observation about grouping; computed contrast, DOM reading order and keyboard reachability need their own evidence.

For Chinese editorial copy, read the visible line endings aloud. Try keeping the subject with its verb in a title, then check at 320px and at enlarged text sizes. Let text wrap if enforced line breaks would clip it. Start the body at a comfortable measure and compare an actual paragraph with its revised setting. Keep punctuation, proper names and captions readable in context.

For imagery, inspect what survives each crop. A thumbnail grid reveals differences across a series. A single wide photograph can establish a point of view but can delay discovery of the other work. Compare desktop and narrow crops of the same source and check the feature on which its meaning depends.

## Change the feeling of an existing screen

Walk through the few seconds around the stiff moment. On a receipt, a person who has just paid may look for the amount and where the receipt went. A short confirmation with the amount and the actual delivery destination can put that question to rest. Show a delivery claim only if the system knows it happened. On an expired recovery link, plain wording and an immediately available resend action can carry the same sense of care with very different copy.

For an editorial piece, try the sequence without new words. View the portrait after the launch pad, then scroll from the human face to the deep field. Compare the scale of the images, the distance between them and where the captions interrupt looking. In a story, read the paragraph before and after a line of dialogue. A separate line or a little breathing room can change how it lands. If the same line now appears twice, keep the instance in the story.

Compare the old and revised moment using the same content and screen width. Describe the change someone can experience, such as returning from a failed refresh to a snapshot whose owner and timestamp are still readable. A claim that this reassures people needs their feedback.

## Render, inspect, revise

Save the initial viewport or a reproducible first draft. Use the same content and viewport for the revision. State the observation, the one change that addresses it, and what is visible afterward. A passing overflow assertion can establish that the viewport fits; someone looking at the picture must still judge whether the subject survived the crop. Keep design-only and critique-only handoffs proportional to the request.
